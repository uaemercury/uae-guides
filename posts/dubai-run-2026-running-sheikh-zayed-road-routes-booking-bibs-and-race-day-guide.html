UAE MERCURY — Your Window to the Emirates

Once a year, Dubai hands its busiest highway to runners. On Sunday 22 November 2026, Sheikh Zayed Road closes to traffic from 6:30am for Dubai Run, the world's largest free community run and the flagship event of Dubai Fitness Challenge. In 2025, 307,000 people took part. There is no entry fee, no race clock and no winner — just a closed motorway, two routes and a city running together. For the complete guide with the current booking status, see the full Dubai Run breakdown.

The date and the start

Dubai Run 2026 takes place on Sunday 22 November 2026, the penultimate weekend of Dubai Fitness Challenge. Runners set off from 6:30am in the start slot chosen during registration, the start line closes at 8:00am and the event wraps up by 9:00am, before Sheikh Zayed Road reopens. Both routes start on Sheikh Zayed Road beside Museum of the Future.

Arrive well before your slot. Road closures and crowds around the start area build up from around 5:30am, and the morning rush around the start is the one part of the day that feels busy rather than joyful. Driving to the start is a bad idea — the road is closed, and the surrounding streets are gridlocked. The official guidance is to take the Metro: World Trade Centre station for the 5km route and Emirates Towers station for the 10km route. Keep at least AED 15 balance on your nol card or app so you are not stuck topping up in the morning rush.

The two routes: 5km vs 10km

The 5km route is the family route. It starts on Sheikh Zayed Road by Museum of the Future and finishes on Sheikh Mohammed bin Rashid Boulevard near Dubai Mall. Flat, relaxed and the most popular choice for families, first-timers and walkers, it is the fun run most people mean when they talk about Dubai Run. Plenty of participants walk the whole way in run T-shirts, taking photos of the skyline.

The 10km route is the serious one. It also starts by Museum of the Future, continues along Sheikh Zayed Road and finishes at the DIFC Gate Building near Emirates Towers. It is the best option if you want a proper training run on a completely closed Sheikh Zayed Road, with the city's towers on both sides for the whole distance.

Both routes are untimed: there is no official race clock, no winner and no pressure. Run, jog or walk the whole way. That is the whole point of the event — it is a community fitness celebration, not a competition.

Registration: free, compulsory, and not yet open

Registration is free, online only, and compulsory. There is no on-the-day registration and you cannot take part without a bib. As of 3 November 2026, Dubai Run booking had not opened yet — the separate general Dubai Fitness Challenge sign-up is open, and Dubai Ride booking opened first. When Run booking opens on the official Dubai Run page or your DFC account, the steps are straightforward: log in to your Dubai Fitness Challenge account, open the Dubai Run event booking, choose your route, pick a start-time slot, register every participant individually, select whether you want the official T-shirt, and save your confirmation and QR code.

Two details catch people out. First, registering for the challenge does not book you into the run — the flagship events each need a separate booking on top of the general sign-up. Second, a route change means cancelling your registration and re-registering, so choose carefully. Popular early start slots for the 10km typically fill first, so book as soon as it opens rather than waiting for November. Check the full guide for the current status, because it is updated the moment booking opens.

Bib collection: Zabeel Park, 2–21 November

There is no race-day bib collection. Collect your bib — and your T-shirt, if you selected one during registration — from the Dubai Municipality 30×30 Fitness Village at Zabeel Park between 2 and 21 November 2026. Bring your registration QR code. If you cannot go yourself, someone else can collect for you using your QR code, which is handy if you registered the whole family. Parents and guardians can register up to three children under "My family" in their DFC account.

Children of all ages can take part; a parent or legal guardian registers them and accepts the waiver, and under-18s must be accompanied by an adult on the route. Walking is completely fine, and pets are not allowed on the route. The bib is your entry ticket — without it, you cannot join the run, so treat the collection window as part of the event, not an afterthought.

Dubai Run in context

Dubai Run sits inside the Dubai Fitness Challenge 2026, the month-long 30×30 movement that runs from 31 October to 29 November 2026. The challenge, launched in 2017 by Sheikh Hamdan bin Mohammed bin Rashid Al Maktoum, asks everyone for 30 minutes of activity every day for 30 days, and the 2026 edition is the tenth. More than 16 million people have taken part across the first nine editions. Inside that month sit four flagship events: Dubai Ride on 1 November, Stand Up Paddle on 7–8 November, Dubai Run on 22 November and Dubai Yoga on 29 November.

The 2025 edition, held on 23 November 2025, drew 307,000 participants onto Sheikh Zayed Road according to Dubai Media Office figures — a sea of runners between the skyscrapers that has become one of Dubai's defining images. The run has grown every year since it began, and the 2026 edition is expected to be bigger still.

Race-day tips

Wear comfortable shoes you have already broken in, and dress for a cool morning that warms up fast — a light layer you can tie around your waist works well. Bring water, though water stations are provided along the route, and sunscreen if you are on the later start slots. Agree a meeting point with your group in advance, because mobile signal can be patchy with hundreds of thousands of people in one place. And take the Metro home too: the roads reopen gradually, taxis are scarce, and the stations are the fastest way out. The run ends by 9am, which leaves the whole Sunday free — most people head for brunch, and the city feels like it is still celebrating for hours afterwards.
