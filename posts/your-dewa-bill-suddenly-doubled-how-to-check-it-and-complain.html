Your DEWA Bill Suddenly Doubled? How to Check It and Complain

A DEWA bill that suddenly doubles is one of the most stressful surprises in Dubai life — and the first thing to know is that most "wrong bill" cases turn out to be real consumption. Before you complain, compare this month's electricity kWh and water gallons with your previous bills. Not the dirham total — the units. Summer air conditioning is the usual cause: a thermostat set just 1–2°C lower across a month adds hundreds of kWh, and DEWA charges more per unit as consumption rises, through tariff slabs of 23, then 28, then 32, then 38 fils per kWh once you cross 2,000, 4,000 and 6,000 kWh. Water slabs climb the same way, from 3.5 to 4.0 to 4.6 fils per gallon.

Rule out the usual suspects first

A silently running toilet or a dripping tap can add thousands of gallons. New appliances — a water heater, a dryer, an extra fridge or freezer — are heavy consumers. An estimated reading, when DEWA could not read your meter, can also look like a spike; the next actual reading corrects it. And a renewed tenancy at a higher rent raises the housing fee automatically, since it is billed at 5% of annual rent each month. Check the meter reading dates too — a longer billing period explains a bigger bill. If the units jumped with no lifestyle change, you have a genuine case. If only the dirhams jumped, check the slabs and fees first.

How to file the complaint

DEWA's official service is called "Request for consumption verification — electricity/water", and it is free. The fastest route is online: go to dewa.gov.ae or open the DEWA Smart App, log in with your DEWA ID or UAE Pass, navigate to Consumption Management, then "Request for consumption verification". Fill in the form with your contract account number and contact details, and choose one of three options: self-verification, a guided set of checks you do yourself; a callback from a DEWA agent; or an onsite visit. You receive a notification number by SMS and email to track the request. You can also call the Customer Care Centre on 04-6019999, write to the customer care centre with your contract account number, or walk into any DEWA Customer Happiness Centre with the account holder's valid ID. For electrical emergencies, the number is 991.

Fees, timelines and the meter test

Online and app complaints are free, and so are self-verification and agent callbacks. DEWA resolves complaints within 7 working days online or through the app, and in about 3 working days via a Happiness Centre. An onsite meter test costs AED 30 for a small meter and AED 75 for a large meter — but only if the meter turns out to be working fine. If the test finds the meter faulty, you pay nothing, and DEWA recalculates and adjusts your previous bills. Thukher cardholders get a 50% discount on service fees.

What happens after you complain

DEWA acknowledges your request with a tracking number. For onsite visits, technicians test your meter against calibrated standards — water meters may be lab-tested, with a temporary replacement installed while yours is checked. If a fault is found, your bills are recalculated, adjustments are credited, and no inspection fee is charged. If the meter is fine, the test fee applies and the bill stands — which is why the self-checks first, comparing units across three months and ruling out leaks, new appliances and slab jumps, save most people a wasted visit.
