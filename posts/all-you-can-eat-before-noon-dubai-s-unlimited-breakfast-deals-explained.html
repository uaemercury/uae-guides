There is a particular pleasure in a breakfast with no natural end — no single plate, no polite portion, just a buffet counter or a menu that keeps producing food until you surrender. Dubai, a city that loves an all-you-can-eat format, has quietly built a whole market of these breakfasts under AED 99, and the bottom of that market is startling: the cheapest verified unlimited breakfast in the city costs AED 18. Before you book one, though, it pays to understand that "unlimited" means three different things in Dubai, and the format you choose decides whether the deal is genuinely good value.

The three formats, explained

The first format is the classic self-serve buffet: you walk up to the counter as many times as you like, and the only limit is the closing time. This is how hotel breakfasts work, and also how the cheapest deals in the city work. The second format is the unlimited table-served menu: there is no counter, you order dish after dish from a fixed breakfast menu, and the kitchen keeps bringing plates. This format almost always carries a time limit — usually two hours — and the clock starts when you sit down, not when the first dish arrives. The third format is the weekend brunch-buffet, a longer, later cousin that runs to 3pm or 4pm and blends breakfast into lunch. If you sleep in on Saturdays, this is your format. Confusing the three is how people end up disappointed: a two-hour table-served deal is a fine thing, but it is not a lazy three-hour buffet.

The under-AED-50 league

Dubai's cheapest unlimited breakfasts are all Indian, Lebanese or Turkish, and all under AED 50 a head. A Kerala breakfast buffet — puttu, the steamed rice flour and coconut cylinders eaten with curries, alongside dosa and idli — is advertised at AED 18 a person, served daily from 7am to 11am. An unlimited Lebanese buffet of hummus, foul, labneh and fresh bread in Discovery Gardens costs AED 19. A weekend vegetarian Indian breakfast buffet runs AED 21. In Dubai Marina, of all places, an unlimited brunch buffet with an egg station and pastries is reported at AED 45 — remarkable for the postcode, though Marina venues reprice often, so call ahead. A no-frills weekend fry-up in Al Quoz matches that AED 45, Friday to Sunday until 12:30pm. And the best-known name in this league is the Turkish breakfast: a table covered in small dishes — cheeses, olives, eggs, dips, fresh bread — starting from AED 49 for nine dishes, with 18-dish and 36-dish versions above it, served all day from 8am to 4pm. Because it runs all day, it doubles as a late breakfast or early lunch. Near the airport, an Indian breakfast buffet at AED 49 with chai, poha, upma, dosa and idli covers the early-flight crowd.

The sweet spot: AED 50 to 75

Between AED 50 and 75 the deals get longer hours, hotel settings and wider menus. A café inside a Marina hotel charges AED 50 for a weekday breakfast buffet that turns into an unlimited chef's selection until 4pm at weekends. A budget hotel at the airport lists its full breakfast buffet at AED 50 — the cheapest reliable hotel buffet in the city. An Italian all-you-can-eat breakfast in the Marina runs daily, not just weekends, at AED 65, which makes it the most flexible deal for weekday visitors. Several café names cluster at AED 69: two hours of unlimited selected dishes at a coffee chain, an unlimited weekend menu of eggs Benedict, shakshuka and Turkish eggs in JLT, and a weekend deal that includes one drink — a detail that matters, because at rival cafés a coffee or juice added separately can put AED 20 to 30 on the bill. A Mexican all-you-can-eat Sunday brunch, also AED 69, runs latest of all, from 11am to 5pm on a Marina terrace. The crowd favourite at AED 74 is a café brunch with more than 25 dishes plus unlimited tea or coffee, running 8am to 4pm across multiple locations on weekends — note that older listings still show AED 69, so budget for the current figure.

When the extra buys something real

From AED 75 to 99, the premium is not really about more food; it buys a setting or an extra. One hotel in Jumeirah Village pairs its AED 75 breakfast buffet, with live cooking stations and unlimited juices, with pool access — breakfast becomes a half-day out with no hotel booking required. A Downtown hotel buffet at AED 75 covers the Dubai Mall end of town. At AED 79, weekend deals add a specialty coffee or fresh juice into the price, and a mall-based Italian chain serves its all-you-can-eat breakfast daily, which fits neatly around shopping. The lesson of the whole range is that price and quality do not climb together in a straight line: the AED 19 Lebanese buffet and the AED 95 healthy-breakfast spread are both good at what they do, and the right choice depends on whether your morning is about fuel or about the occasion.

A final practical word: buffet prices in Dubai change with the season, and most venues add 5% VAT on top of the advertised figure. A thirty-second phone call before you travel is the difference between a deal and a surprise — especially at the neighbourhood spots, whose hours shift more often than the hotels'.
