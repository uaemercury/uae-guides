UAE MERCURY — Your Window to the Emirates

There is a perfect food walk hiding in plain sight in Dubai, and it costs less than a taxi across town. It runs from Deira's docks through the souks to the Al Fahidi wind towers, powered by a one-dirham boat ride. Do it on a winter morning, hungry, and it will rearrange what you think Dubai food is.

Start: Baniyas Road and the boats

Begin on the Deira side of the Creek, where wooden dhows still load cargo for Iran, India and East Africa, much as they have for a century. The cafeterias serving the port area open early: tea first — a karak, strong and sweet — then look for a bakery window selling khameer bread hot from the oven, or a counter with chebab pancakes and cream cheese. This is breakfast as port workers eat it: fast, cheap and very good.

The spice souk detour

Walk five minutes into the Spice Souk and slow down. Sacks of dried black limes (loomi), saffron, bezar spice blends and dried roses perfume the lanes — the exact ingredients you are about to taste cooked. Stallholders will offer tastes and tea; buy a small bag of bezar to take home, because by the end of this walk you will want to cook with it.

The one-dirham crossing

At the abra station, join the queue for the wooden water taxis that shuttle across the Creek for a single dirham. Sit sideways, watch the minarets and dhows slide past, and enjoy the best-value transport experience in the entire Gulf. Two minutes later you are in Bur Dubai.

Bur Dubai: the heritage lunch

Climb up from the abra dock into Al Fahidi, the wind-tower quarter. This is the place to sit down properly: a courtyard restaurant, a shared platter of machboos or a bowl of harees, luqaimat arriving hot with date syrup, and gahwa poured the ceremonial way. Book the cultural-centre meals ahead if your dates allow — the hosted format turns lunch into the best history lesson in the city.

The sweet finish

Walk it off through the Textile Souk and along the Creek to Al Seef, stopping wherever luqaimat or khanfaroosh are frying. End at a Creek-side café at golden hour, when the abras multiply and the old town does its sunset performance.

Practical notes

  • Go between November and March if you can; summer walking is a sport, not a stroll.
  • Carry small cash for abras, bakeries and souk stalls.
  • Total food spend for a generous version of this walk: well under AED 120.
  • Dress modestly — you are in the city's most traditional districts.

One creek, two banks, a thousand years of trade in every bite: this is the Dubai food walk that matters.

Walk logistics

Start around 9 a.m. in winter and you will finish by two with lunch done, before the afternoon haze softens the light. The full route is barely four kilometres of walking, broken by the abra ride and long café stops — manageable for most fitness levels and genuinely pleasant in the December-to-February window. Bring a bottle of water anyway, a small backpack for souk purchases, and shoes you have already broken in; the old town's lanes alternate between smooth stone and gloriously uneven history. Photographers: the Creek faces east on the Deira side, so morning light flatters the dhows, while Al Fahidi's courtyards glow in the hour before sunset if you linger.

End where you began, by the water, as the light fails and the old town lights its lanterns — the walk makes a circle, and so does the appetite.
