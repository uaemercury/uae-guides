UAE MERCURY — Your Window to the Emirates

Dubai's restaurant scene can feed you food from a hundred countries in one evening, which makes it oddly easy to leave without ever tasting the food of the Emirates itself. Emirati cooking is gentle, aromatic and deeply hospitable — built on rice, slow-cooked meats, dried limes, saffron and dates. If you are starting from zero, these ten dishes are the way in.

1. Machboos

The national centrepiece: spiced rice cooked with meat (chicken, lamb or fish), dried black lime (loomi) and a warm blend of spices similar to a biryani's cousin. Every family guards its own version. If you try one dish, make it this one.

2. Harees

Wheat and meat beaten together into a smooth, porridge-like dish, traditionally served during Ramadan and at celebrations. Mild, comforting and far more than the sum of its parts — especially with a drizzle of ghee on top.

3. Balaleet

The beloved breakfast surprise: sweet vermicelli noodles scented with cardamom and saffron, topped with a thin omelette. Sweet and savoury in the same bite. Order it once and you will order it forever.

4. Luqaimat

Golden, crisp dumplings soaked in date syrup (dibs), served hot. The UAE's favourite sweet — you will find them at festivals, in malls and at every traditional restaurant worth its name. Eat them within minutes of frying.

5. Salona

A home-style stew of meat or chicken with vegetables in a tomato-based sauce, served over rice. This is everyday Emirati cooking — the dish grandmothers make, rarely the one on glossy menus, and all the better for it.

6. Thareed

Bread layered at the bottom of a bowl and soaked with meat and vegetable stew until it softens into the sauce. Ancient, filling and famously a favourite of the Prophet's era. Rare in restaurants; grab it if you see it.

7. Chebab

Emirati pancakes — soft, golden, saffron-and-cardamom scented — eaten with cream cheese (the modern habit) or date syrup. Breakfast and dessert in one.

8. Madrooba

Salted fish or chicken pounded with rice into a rich paste. An acquired taste for some, a point of pride for coastal families. The fish version connects the plate directly to the old pearl-diving coast.

9. Ghuzi

Whole roasted lamb served over spiced rice with nuts — the celebration dish, made for weddings and honoured guests. In restaurants you will usually find a single-portion version.

10. Dates and Arabic coffee (gahwa)

Not a dish, but the ritual that frames everything: bitter cardamom coffee poured from a dallah pot, with dates to sweeten it. Refusing the first cup is the only real faux pas.

How to order your first Emirati meal

Start with balaleet or chebab if it is morning; machboos for the main; luqaimat and coffee to finish. Ask for the spice level honestly — Emirati food is aromatic rather than fiery, so almost anyone can eat it happily. Ten dishes, one afternoon of eating, and you will understand this country's kitchen better than most people who have lived here for years.

Your first Emirati plate, assembled

If the list above feels like a lot, compress it into one order: machboos for the centre of the table, a small harees to understand the tradition, chebab or balaleet if the hour allows, luqaimat and gahwa to close. That single spread contains the cuisine's whole argument — spice-trade rice, ancient wheat, sweet breakfast logic, and coffee as ceremony. Everything after it is delicious refinement.

Most of all, do not let a hundred other cuisines talk you out of the one that was here first. The Emirates cooked for travellers long before it built hotels for them — sit down at its table at least once.
