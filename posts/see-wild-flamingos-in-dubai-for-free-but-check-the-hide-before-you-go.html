First, the trap: the most-quoted opening hours for Ras Al Khor are out of date. Older guides list 9:00am to 4:00pm, and plenty of pages still repeat them. The hours Dubai Municipality and Visit Dubai currently publish are longer, seasonal, and different again on Fridays. The second trap is bigger: a major redevelopment is under way at the sanctuary, one of its three hides is temporarily closed, and access can change. None of this stops a visit — the flamingos are there and entry is still free — but it does mean you should plan from the current facts, not the copied ones.

The hours that are actually published

In winter, October to March, the hides open Saturday to Thursday from 7:30am to 5:30pm. In summer, April to September, they open from 6:00am to 6:00pm. On Fridays they open at 2:00pm in both seasons, closing at 5:30pm in winter and 6:00pm in summer. Inside those hours sits the number that matters most: the flamingo feeding window, 10:00am to 4:00pm every day, when the birds are most active in the shallows in front of the hides. A 7:30am winter arrival can actually be too early — on cool mornings the birds sometimes appear a little later — while a summer visit should start at the 6:00am opening, because the hides have no air conditioning and the heat builds fast. Late morning, 10:00 to 11:00am, and late afternoon, 3:00 to 4:00pm, are the best slots in any season.

The redevelopment, stated plainly

Dubai Municipality is carrying out a major development project at Ras Al Khor — about AED 650 million in total, with the first phase expected to finish by the end of 2026. In July 2025, Khaleej Times reported that the sanctuary had temporarily closed as part of that work. As of October 2026, the Municipality's booking page still marked the Mangrove Hide as temporarily closed, while listing the Flamingo Hide and the Lagoon Hide without a closure note. The honest summary: the sanctuary's headline experience — flamingos from the Flamingo Hide — was listed as open, the mangrove side was not, and the situation can move. The timings and free entry in this article reflect the latest published information as of 17 November 2026. Confirm the day's opening on the Municipality's protected-areas platform or by calling 800900 before you travel. That single check is the difference between a good morning and a wasted drive.

What is free, and what is asked of you

Entry is free for all ages, and no booking is required for individuals and families — Visit Dubai states the bird hides are free to use. Two qualifications have appeared in recent guides: walk-in registration may be required on arrival, and you may be asked for an Emirates ID, so carry ID. Only 15 people are allowed inside a hide at a time, which means a short wait is possible on a busy winter weekend. Free guided tours run Sunday to Thursday, 7:30am to 2:00pm, bookable by email through Dubai Municipality. Inside the hide, the rules are simple: enter quietly, keep voices low, do not feed the birds, do not leave the hide to get closer, and never use flash or fly a drone over the protected habitat. Binoculars are provided at the hides, though the birds feed at some distance, so bring your own if you have them.

Which hide, and how you get there

All viewing is from three perimeter hides — the wetland itself is fenced off. The Flamingo Hide, on the western edge reached from Oud Metha Road and Ras Al Khor Road (E44) near Dubai Festival City, is the one the Municipality advertises for flamingo viewing all year round, with the closest views of the roost, free binoculars reported, and a small free car park of about 20–25 spaces that fills quickly on winter weekend mornings. The Mangrove Hide overlooks mangroves and mudflats — herons, egrets, spoonbills and waders — and is the closed one, so check before counting on it. The Lagoon Hide overlooks open water for waders, ducks and raptors. Getting there always ends in a taxi or a car: the Green Line to Creek station or Al Jadaf, then about five minutes by taxi; buses 55, 61, 63E, X23 and E16 to Aden Street 1 or the Dubai Country Club stop, then a taxi or a 15-minute walk; or drive via E44 or Oud Metha Road, about 10–15 minutes by taxi from Downtown Dubai or Festival City.

Why it is still worth the check

Because the sight itself has no substitute in the city: greater flamingos feeding in the shallows of a Ramsar-listed wetland — tidal mudflats, salt flats, lagoons and around 47 hectares of mangroves — with the Dubai skyline directly behind them. Winter numbers can reach up to 25,000 birds across 201 species, and December to February is the peak. The verified visiting details — hours, hides, transport and the redevelopment note — are collected in the guide to visiting Ras Al Khor Wildlife Sanctuary.

Check first, arrive inside the feeding window, and the sanctuary does the rest. Skip the check, and you are gambling a morning on last year's information.
