UAE MERCURY — Your Window to the Emirates

Owning a car in Dubai is famously cheap by world standards — fuel costs little, cars are plentiful. And yet a growing number of single residents are choosing not to, because the metro-and-Nol-card life, done properly, costs less per month than car insurance alone. This is how the car-free single life actually works in Dubai.

The Nol card, your whole transport system

One card runs the metro, trams, buses and even some marine transport. Fares are zone-based — most single journeys land between AED 3 and AED 7.50 — and monthly passes exist for heavy users (unlimited-travel passes have historically been priced around AED 140–350 depending on zones and card type; check the current RTA prices, as they change). A typical commuter spending is AED 200–350 a month all-in. Compare that with car ownership: insurance, registration, Salik tolls, parking, fuel and maintenance rarely total under AED 1,200 a month even for a paid-off car.

Living on the line

The car-free life is won or lost by one decision: where you live. Choose a home within a 10-minute walk of a metro station and the city opens up — the Red and Green lines reach the airport, both old towns, the marina, JLT, Business Bay and most employment hubs. Live off the line and you will leak money into taxis until the maths collapses. Singles have an advantage here: studios near stations are exactly the stock the market builds most of.

Filling the gaps

  • Buses extend the network into the districts the metro skips, on the same card, and winter makes waiting at the stop a pleasure rather than a punishment.
  • Taxis and ride-hailing for the late nights and awkward trips: budget a monthly allowance (AED 150–300 covers most singles' overflow) instead of treating each ride as an emergency.
  • E-scooters and bikes cover the last kilometre in the districts that allow them — marina promenades, JLT, Business Bay.
  • Walking, November to March: entire neighbourhoods become walkable; the Creek, the souks and the marina are pedestrian pleasures in season.

The real trade-offs

Summer is the honest cost: June to September makes any walk to a station a sweat event, and the car-free life leans harder on taxis and buses with shade. Shift workers and families have harder equations. But a single person with a 9-to-6 near the line loses surprisingly little — and gains a benefit nobody prices in: no parking hunts, no Salik anxiety, no annual registration week.

The verdict in numbers

Car-free single, living on the line: transport lands around AED 350–600 a month including taxi overflow. Car-owning single: AED 1,200–2,000 before the loan payment. The difference — call it AED 1,000 a month — is a holiday every quarter, or the deposit on next year's studio.

Dubai was built for cars. Its metro quietly lets one person opt out — and pocket the difference.

The hybrid most people actually choose

Reality check: the most common single-person setup in Dubai is neither pure metro nor full car ownership — it is metro-first with a car appearing later, once a promotion or a district move changes the maths. There is no failure in switching; there is only failure in buying the car in month one, before you know your station, your commute and your actual evenings. Rent yourself six car-free months first. The city will tell you whether you miss driving — and for a surprising number of singles, it never does.
