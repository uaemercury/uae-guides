A sound bath sounds mysterious right up until you attend one. Then it becomes simple: you lie on a mat for about an hour while someone plays large singing bowls, gongs and chimes around the room, and the sound does the rest. No poses, no chanting, nothing to get right. Here is what a first session actually looks like, minute by minute.

Arriving and settling in

Sessions usually run in small studios with dim light and mats already laid out, often with a blanket and an eye pillow. You keep your normal clothes on, take your shoes off, and lie down. The facilitator explains briefly what will happen and asks everyone to silence their phones. Most groups are small — eight to fifteen people.

The first ten minutes

The session typically opens with a few minutes of guided breathing, then the first bowls begin. Singing bowls produce a low, layered hum that you feel as much as hear — a mild vibration in the chest and floor. Beginners usually spend these minutes thinking about work. That is normal, and it fades.

The deep middle

As gongs and deeper bowls join, most people drift into a state between waking and sleep. Time becomes unreliable; an hour often feels like twenty minutes. Some people fall fully asleep, which facilitators treat as a perfectly good outcome. Others notice emotions surfacing — also normal, and also treated as part of the process rather than a problem.

Waking up and afterwards

The sounds thin out, a soft chime marks the end, and you sit up slowly with water or tea offered. The common report afterwards is a specific kind of quiet — shoulders down, jaw unclenched, head clear. Effects are usually strongest the same evening's sleep.

If you are curious about trying sound healing in Dubai, sessions run in studios across JLT, Business Bay, Alserkal Avenue and Jumeirah, typically lasting 60 to 90 minutes. Bring nothing but an open mind and arrive ten minutes early. Of all the wellness practices in the city, this is the one that asks the least of you.
