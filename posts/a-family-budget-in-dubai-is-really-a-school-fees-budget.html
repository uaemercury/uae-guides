UAE MERCURY — Your Window to the Emirates

Strip a Dubai family budget down to its skeleton and two bones carry the weight: rent and school fees. Food, transport and utilities wobble by hundreds of dirhams. School fees wobble by thousands — per month. This is the arithmetic of that wobble, and why the curriculum decision matters more than the postcode decision.

Two children, two completely different budgets

Dubai's average school fee is about AED 42,000 per child per year, but the average hides the real story. Indian-curriculum (CBSE/ICSE) schools run AED 6,000–30,000 a year per child, averaging around AED 14,000 — well-known names like The Indian High School sit between roughly AED 5,500 and AED 10,500. British-curriculum schools run AED 15,000–120,000. American schools run AED 30,000–90,000, and IB averages about AED 65,000.

Put two children through it and the monthly maths is stark: two children in a mid-range CBSE school cost roughly AED 2,000–3,000 a month in fees; two in a mid-range British school cost AED 7,000–10,000. Switching curriculum alone can save AED 5,000–7,000 a month — more than most families save by moving house. One relief for planners: KHDA, the regulator, has frozen school fees for the 2026–27 academic year, so the figures you budget now should hold all year. Do not forget the extras, though — the school bus adds AED 4,000–9,000 a year per child, and uniforms, books and activities another AED 1,800–5,500.

Rent is the second lever, not the first

Once schools are chosen, rent sets the floor. A 2-bedroom apartment in International City runs AED 58,000–82,000 a year; JVC, the best all-round family value, runs AED 85,000–115,000; Al Barsha, with the Metro and Mall of the Emirates, runs AED 105,000–130,000. Al Nahda, at about AED 72,000 a year for a 2BR with Green Line access, is the value pick many families miss. Two traps catch new families: a brand-new lease costs more than a renewal (median new 2BR leases sit around AED 84,000 against about AED 65,000 for renewals), and in many towers the cooling is billed separately — district cooling can add AED 800–1,400 a month in summer on top of a DEWA bill of AED 400–800 for a 2BR.

The lines that barely move

Everything else is comparatively well-behaved. Groceries for four run AED 2,500–4,000 a month at mid-range supermarkets, dropping to about AED 2,000–2,500 for families who cook at home from Lulu and local markets. One family car, excluding any loan, costs roughly AED 900–2,200 a month all-in. A mid-tier family health-insurance plan — mandatory, and usually only the employee is covered by the employer — runs about AED 1,800–2,500 a month. Transport behaves too: petrol was AED 4.16 a litre for Super 98 in November 2026, Salik tolls run AED 6 at peak and AED 4 off-peak (a regular commuter pays roughly AED 200–550 a month), and a monthly Nol pass at about AED 300–400 can replace a second car entirely for school runs near a station. These lines matter, but they argue in hundreds, not thousands.

Run your own total

Add your version up and you will land in one of three bands: a careful budget family at AED 13,000–19,000 a month, a mid-range family at AED 21,000–33,000, or a comfortable villa-and-top-schools life at AED 40,000–60,000. Decide the schools first, then the street, then everything else. In Dubai, the family budget is written in that order whether you plan it that way or not.
