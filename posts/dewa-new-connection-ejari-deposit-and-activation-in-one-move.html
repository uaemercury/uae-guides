DEWA New Connection: Ejari, Deposit and Activation in One Move

Moving into a new home in Dubai comes with a long checklist, and switching on the electricity and water sits near the top of it. The good news is that for most tenants, a DEWA new connection now happens almost by itself. DEWA's "One Step Move-in" links directly to Ejari registration, so when your tenancy contract is registered, DEWA receives your details automatically and opens an account — no separate application in most cases.

Here is how it works. Your landlord or agent registers the tenancy and the Ejari certificate is issued. DEWA's system picks up your details automatically, and you receive a welcome email and SMS with your new DEWA account number, the security deposit amount and a payment link. Pay the deposit and the activation fees online, and electricity and water connect within 15 working hours of payment.

What it costs

The security deposit is AED 2,000 for apartments and AED 4,000 for villas, plus around AED 155 in activation fees. That deposit is refundable when you eventually move out and close the account, so keep the account number and payment receipt somewhere safe. The activation fee covers the cost of connecting supply and setting up the account.

When it is not automatic

The automatic route covers most tenants, but some cases still need a manual application: owners applying for their own property, free-zone properties and commercial units. Owners apply manually online with a Title Deed. If your case is not automatic, apply as early as you can — the 15-working-hour connection clock only starts once payment is made, so a late application can mean a first night in a dark flat.

A few practical points make the move-in smoother. Register Ejari promptly, because that is what triggers the automatic account. Check the welcome email and SMS as soon as they arrive — the payment link inside them is the fastest way to pay the deposit. And once supply is connected, note your account number: every DEWA service afterwards, from checking a bill to paying it, asks for the same 10-digit number.
