Going Solo in the UAE: The Cheapest Freelance Permits Ranked for 2026

Working for yourself in the Emirates starts with one legal foundation: a freelance permit. It is the document that lets you contract with clients, issue invoices, and — when paired with a residence visa — live in the country while you build your business. The freelance visa UAE route looks simple at first glance, but advertised prices hide a stack of add-ons, and the difference between a permit and a visa is where most newcomers lose money. This guide covers the real costs, the cheapest zones in each emirate, and the application steps.

Permit versus visa: the distinction that matters most

In the UAE, the freelance permit and the residence visa are two separate purchases. The permit is your licence to work — the legal right to contract and invoice clients. The residence visa is your right to live here, processed through immigration on the strength of that permit plus an establishment card (around AED 2,000 a year), which acts as the bridge between the free zone and immigration.

An advertised AED 7,500 "freelance visa" almost always means the permit alone: a realistic first year in Dubai lands at roughly AED 12,800–14,600 before medical, Emirates ID, and insurance (one published breakdown lands at exactly AED 12,830). Budget the stack, not the headline.

The GoFreelance route in Dubai

The cheapest freelance permit issued inside Dubai costs AED 7,500 a year through GoFreelance, run by Dubai's TECOM Group and handled through the AXS portal. That figure is published by the authority itself and confirmed across multiple 2026 comparison guides.

The AED 7,500 covers a one-year permit across more than 100 activities in media, technology, education, and design, valid across Dubai's TECOM zones. It does not cover the residence visa, establishment card, medical, Emirates ID, or health insurance. If you already live in the UAE on a spouse's or employer's sponsorship, the permit alone is enough — plus your sponsor's No Objection Certificate — which makes GoFreelance the cheapest Dubai option for resident side-freelancers by a wide margin.

The full first-year breakdown in Dubai

Published 2026 figures put the typical all-in first year at AED 12,830–16,000:

  • GoFreelance freelance permit: AED 7,500, annual
  • Establishment card: around AED 2,000, annual
  • Two-year residence visa: AED 3,330–5,042, every two years
  • Medical fitness test: AED 300–1,200, per visa
  • Emirates ID: AED 370–470, per visa
  • Health insurance (mandatory, DHA-compliant): AED 800–3,000, annual

A one-year visa is advertised at AED 4,600 if you would rather keep the first commitment short. Do not skip the insurance: a missing DHA-compliant policy is among the most common reasons visa stamping gets delayed.

Dubai Media City sells pricier freelance packages (roughly AED 11,000–15,000 a year for the permit) — only worth it if a client requires the DMC brand, since GoFreelance already covers its activities.

Cheaper options outside Dubai

Outside Dubai the numbers drop sharply, and all figures below are published package prices — the authority confirms the final itemisation before you pay.

Ajman (NuVentures Centre) advertises the country's lowest headline: around AED 4,888 for a permit-only licence, roughly AED 10,800 with a visa. Fujairah Creative City follows at about AED 4,900, roughly AED 12,500 with a visa, and suits media and creative freelancers. SHAMS Sharjah offers a permit from roughly AED 5,750 with permit plus two-year visa packages from AED 7,500–10,000 — often the cheapest permit-plus-visa combo and the cheapest two-year total. RAKEZ Ras Al Khaimah starts at about AED 6,100 with a permit plus visa first year of AED 8,500–14,000, with straightforward setup and banking reported to be easier than with some of the cheapest emirate zones. Abu Dhabi's twofour54 is the media specialist's pick at roughly AED 3,539 for the permit. IFZA Dubai ranges AED 7,500–12,900 on the permit, with a published one-visa first year from around AED 13,050.

Two caveats: banks are reported to reject more Ajman licences than other emirates, and IFZA pricing varies by source (one breakdown lands near AED 13,050 while list-price packages reach AED 25,970 and beyond) — always get the itemised written quote from the zone itself. If you are weighing a freelance permit against a small company setup, the cheapest free zone licence comparison is worth reading.

Federal alternatives worth knowing

The MOHRE freelance work permit, published at around AED 1,200–2,500 for existing UAE residents, leaves their sponsorship untouched — the cheapest side-freelancing route, though not a shortcut to a residence visa, which it does not grant. And the AED 1,070 e-Trader licence includes no visa and no physical shop: it suits online selling through social platforms, not a freelance visa.

How to apply: the GoFreelance steps

The process runs through the AXS portal: pick your zone and activity (media roles usually do not need an attested degree; consulting, IT, and education roles typically do), submit your documents online — passport copy, photo, CV and portfolio, three months of bank statements, and a No Objection Certificate if employer-sponsored — and expect approval in five to ten working days. Pay the AED 7,500 permit fee (residents on existing visas stop here); everyone else then opens an establishment card (around AED 2,000), handles the entry permit and any status change (around AED 1,150–1,600), completes the medical (AED 300–1,200) and Emirates ID biometrics (AED 370–470), and arranges mandatory insurance from around AED 800 a year. Application to Emirates ID in hand: two to four weeks.

Renewal and family sponsorship

Free-zone residence visas are typically issued for two years while the permit renews annually, so a "two-year freelance visa" means one two-year visa plus two years of permit renewals. SHAMS Sharjah often has the cheapest two-year total at AED 7,500–10,000 for permit plus two-year visa in year one; GoFreelance Dubai runs roughly AED 20,000–25,000 all-in over two years.

The permit renews annually (AED 7,500 for GoFreelance) while the visa renews every two years with fresh medical and Emirates ID fees — typical annual renewals run AED 7,000–12,000, so set a reminder thirty days before each expiry. A freelance residence visa holder can sponsor a spouse and children, typically needing around AED 10,000 or more in monthly income and a registered Ejari tenancy contract.

The bottom line

Resident freelancing on the side? The MOHRE permit (~AED 1,200–2,500). Residency at the lowest total cost? SHAMS or Ajman (~AED 7,500–11,000 all-in for year one), with Ajman's banking trade-off in mind. Residency with a Dubai address? GoFreelance's AED 7,500 permit — and apply directly through the zone's official portal, since consultants add their own markup.
