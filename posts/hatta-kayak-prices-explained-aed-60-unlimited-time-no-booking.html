Hatta Kayak Prices Explained: AED 60, Unlimited Time, No Booking

Most water activities in the UAE are priced by the hour, metered like a taxi. Hatta Dam is the exception. The hatta kayak price is AED 60 for a single kayak and AED 120 for a double — and that is a flat fee for unlimited time on the water, not a 60-minute rental. Pay once, paddle as long as you like, come back when you are done. It is a big part of why kayaking here has become one of the cheapest and most popular outdoor activities in the country. This explainer walks through the full price picture: who can ride, what else is for rent, when to go, and the rules that catch visitors out.

The two prices that matter

A single kayak costs AED 60. It takes one adult: you must be 16 or older and weigh no more than 110 kg. A double kayak costs AED 120 and takes two adults plus one child aged 2 to 4, with the same 110 kg limit per person. Do the maths and there is no saving either way — two singles cost exactly the same as one double — so choose on comfort. Solo paddlers and confident pairs go faster and steer more easily in singles; couples, nervous paddlers and anyone with a small child should take the double. Under-16s cannot take a single kayak alone, and infants are not allowed on the water at all.

Hatta Kayak is the only operator permitted on the reservoir. Private kayaks and paddleboards are not allowed, so there is no cheaper do-it-yourself option — but at AED 60, there does not need to be one.

Flat fee, not per hour — ignore the blogs that say otherwise

Several travel blogs describe the price as AED 60 to 75 per hour. That is wrong. The operator's own model is a flat fee, confirmed on the official price table and on-site boards: you pay once and the boat is yours until you bring it back. In practice, most people paddle for 60 to 90 minutes — long enough to cross the reservoir, drift back slowly and take the photos — but nobody is watching a clock, and there is no extra charge if you stay out longer. Staff will call boats in if the wind picks up or the light fades, which is the only real limit on your session.

What else is for rent at the dam

Kayaks are the main event, but the same counter rents pedal boats — single AED 60, double AED 120 and triple AED 180, with a 75 kg limit per person — which suit visitors who would rather pedal than paddle, especially with children. Larger options include a donut boat for four adults and two children under 10, a tour boat for three adults and one child, and a solar boat. Those prices are not published anywhere verifiable, so treat them as expected and ask at the counter before you commit. The complete price table with every boat type and rule is worth a read before you go.

No booking — how the system actually works

You do not book a kayak. There is no online ticket and no reservation system for individuals: you drive to Hatta Dam, pay at the counter and take a boat. The fleet is large — more than 100 kayaks — so on a normal day you will be on the water within minutes. Only groups of 50 or more need to reserve in advance. The exception is weekends: from Friday to Sunday the queue builds late morning and the car park fills. Arrive early, or walk up to the viewpoint first and come back — the line moves steadily because boats keep returning.

Timings — and one genuine conflict

The official pages disagree with each other, so plan carefully. The Visit Hatta kayak page lists daily hours of 7:00 AM to 9:00 PM. The Wadi Hub page and recent visitor reports describe split winter hours instead: Monday to Thursday 7 to 11 AM and 3 to 9 PM, with a midday break, and Friday to Sunday 7 AM to 9 PM. Treat the split hours as current for winter and confirm by phone on the morning you travel. Whenever you go, the best water is in the first hour after opening and the last hour before sunset, when the light turns the reservoir turquoise and the mountains throw long shadows. Night rides run too, with some areas restricted after dark.

Rules that catch people out

Life jackets are mandatory and provided free; staff will not let you launch without one, however strong a swimmer you are. Swimming in the reservoir is banned by law — it is drinking water. Fishing is banned, getting out of the boat mid-water is banned, and climbing on the dam structure is banned. Dogs are welcome, but only wearing a life vest and staying inside the kayak. Parking at the dam is free, and free lockers hold your bag and keys while you paddle. Keep your phone in a waterproof pouch: the water is calm, but a phone dropped in a drinking-water reservoir is gone for good.

Is it worth AED 60?

Yes — comfortably. Sixty dirhams buys unlimited time on one of the most photographed stretches of water in the UAE, ringed by the Hajar Mountains, with no booking, no time pressure and no upsell. Compared with an hour of almost any other rental activity in Dubai, it is not really a contest.
