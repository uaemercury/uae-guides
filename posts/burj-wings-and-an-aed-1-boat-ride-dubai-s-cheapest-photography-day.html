UAE MERCURY — Your Window to the Emirates

Total budget for a full day of Dubai photography: one dirham. That is the abra fare across the Creek — and even that has a free workaround if you stay on one bank. The towers, beaches, old town and marina that fill every feed are public space. This is the one-dirham itinerary, plus the rules that keep you legal.

The free icons

Start Downtown. The Burj Khalifa from ground level is free and is the frame most visitors actually want — the tower reflected in Burj Lake or shot from the Souk Al Bahar bridge at blue hour, 20–30 minutes after sunset, when the fountains run roughly every half hour from about 6pm. A few minutes away on Burj Plaza, the Wings of Mexico sculpture is free and open 24/7: stand between the wings with the tower behind you, and go early in the morning, because the sunset queue is real. Both are at Burj Khalifa / Dubai Mall Metro station.

Kite Beach gives you the Burj Al Arab from the sand — free, best early, when wet sand at the tide line mirrors the sail. Al Fahidi's wind-tower lanes are free and best at dawn; Al Seef, next door on the Creek, is best in the evening. The abra crossing between Bur Dubai and Deira costs AED 1 in cash (AED 2 for the newer seated service) and delivers the lowest, most atmospheric shot in the city. Dubai Creek Harbour's viewing point, the Marina and JBR promenades, The Pointe's boardwalk facing Atlantis, and the Museum of the Future's calligraphy facade at dusk: all free. Two free outliers reward a drive: the Ras Al Khor flamingo hides (best November to February, 8–10:30am) and the Al Qudra Love Lakes at sunrise — twin heart-shaped lakes, open 24 hours, no facilities, so bring water.

The paid few, honestly priced

Going up the Burj Khalifa (At The Top, levels 124/125) starts from AED 189 on the official booking site — older guides quoting AED 173 are out of date, and sunset slots sell out days ahead. The View at The Palm starts from AED 115. The Dubai Frame costs AED 50 to go up, but the classic shot of it is free from the Zabeel Park lawns at golden hour (park entry AED 5). Seasonal giants: Miracle Garden at AED 100 and Global Village at AED 25–30, both winter-season only.

The 2026 warnings

Three updates matter. The Museum of the Future itself is closed for an exhibition overhaul since 1 September 2026, reopening expected in early 2027 — the facade remains a free stop, the interior does not exist for now. Ain Dubai was shut "for safety reasons" from March 2026 with no confirmed reopening; photograph it from the Bluewaters promenade for free and check before planning a ride (from AED 145). And La Mer is gone — demolished and redeveloped; do not build a day around it. The Burj Al Arab is also closed for restoration (since April 2026, reopening expected around October 2027) — but the exterior sail is the photograph anyway, and from Kite Beach or the Madinat bridges it photographs exactly as it always did. Finally, drones: recreational flying in Dubai has been suspended since March 2026, so leave the drone at the hotel and shoot from the ground like everyone else.
