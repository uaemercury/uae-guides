How to View Your DEWA Bill Online Before the Due Date

If you live in Dubai, your electricity and water bill comes from DEWA, the Dubai Electricity and Water Authority. The good news is that you no longer need to wait for a paper bill or visit a customer centre to see what you owe. You can pull up the full bill on your phone or computer in about a minute.

What you need
All you need is your DEWA account number, the nine-digit number printed at the top of every bill. It also appears in the monthly email and SMS alerts DEWA sends, and inside the DEWA app if you have used it before. No account number, no enquiry — so it is worth keeping a photo of one bill on your phone.

Check on the DEWA website
The fastest route is the official DEWA website.
1. Open dewa.gov.ae and choose the bill enquiry service.
2. Enter your account number.
3. Your current bill appears, with the amount due, the due date and a breakdown of electricity, water and other charges.
From the same screen you can download a PDF copy or pay immediately by card. You do not need to register for a quick enquiry, but a free DEWA online account also shows your payment history and monthly consumption graphs.

Use the app instead
DEWA's smart app for iOS and Android does everything the website does: view the bill, check past bills, pay, and get a reminder before the due date. Many residents simply use the DubaiNow app, which pulls the DEWA balance in with other Dubai government bills. Most major UAE bank apps can fetch and pay the bill too — just add DEWA as a biller once, using the same account number.

No internet? Other ways to check
If you are offline, you are still covered. DEWA offers an SMS enquiry service for bill details, and payments are accepted at ATMs, exchange houses and DEWA customer happiness centres across Dubai. Signing up for the Green Bill, DEWA's paperless option, means the full bill lands in your email inbox every month automatically.

Two habits that help
Set a phone reminder a few days before the due date; a missed payment can end in disconnection and a reconnection fee. And if the amount suddenly looks much higher than usual, check the consumption section first — a spike usually traces back to air-conditioning use in the summer months or a hidden water leak.
