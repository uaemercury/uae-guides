First, the trap: the most famous 'running' name in Dubai, Al Qudra, is an 86-kilometre cycling track where running is not the done thing at all. The second trap: two of the prettiest park loops charge entry. Dubai's running scene is superb and almost entirely free — provided you know which track is for whom. Consider this the version with the traps marked.

The long run: Jumeirah Corniche

The Jumeirah Corniche is the city's long-run route: 7 km one way, 14 km out-and-back, from Burj Al Arab and Umm Suqeim to Jumeirah 1. The jogging track is 4 metres wide beside a 5-metre walkway, with a separate 16 km cycling track alongside, fitness stations roughly every 500 metres, and cafés, showers and an outdoor gym at Kite Beach. It is LED-lit and open 24/7, with free parking at Kite Beach. One correction to most guides: the 14 km is the whole Corniche — Kite Beach is one section of it, not the full distance. From November to March you can run it at almost any hour; in summer it becomes a dawn-and-after-dark track.

Night owls and beginners

Nad Al Sheba Cycle Park, a converted camel-racing track, runs 4, 6 and 8 km loops on wide smooth asphalt, floodlit until midnight, with air-conditioned changing rooms, showers and free parking — the best night-training option in the city, with running paths alongside the cycle loops. Beginners get two perfect classrooms: Al Barsha Pond Park's 1.5 km rubberised loop, markers every 100 metres, track open from 5am, entry free; and Al Ittihad Park's 3.2 km cushioned loop on the Palm, open 24 hours, also free. The Dubai Water Canal splits the difference — a 3 km dedicated running track inside a 6.4 km waterfront promenade, smart-lit, with cafés along the way.

The scenic middle distances

Two more routes deserve a place in the rotation. The Palm Jumeirah Boardwalk runs 11 km along the crescent — paved, 6 metres wide, open 24/7, with food trucks and benches roughly every 500 metres; parking near Atlantis is paid, and cycling on the boardwalk is disputed, so treat it as runners and walkers only. Dubai Marina Walk is a roughly 7 km loop around the canal — flat, palm-lined and very well lit, with Metro and tram access at both ends; it is a shared path, so save it for easy evening miles rather than intervals. And for race-pace work, The Track at Meydan Golf is a 5 km loop with a professional surface, distance markers and water stations — running only, with free parking on site.

The honest exceptions

Al Qudra's 86 km desert network — a 50 km main loop and the 17.5 km "Stick" — is cycling only; runners should admire it from the car park. Bike rental at the trailhead runs about AED 75 for four hours. Three park tracks charge small entries by Nol card: Zabeel Park (AED 5, 2.5 km perimeter loop, LED-lit), Safa Park (AED 3, roughly 3.5 km around the lake) and Mushrif Park (AED 3 per person or AED 10 per car), which is the mountain-bike and trail option with a 50 km MTB network. Everything else in this article is free.
