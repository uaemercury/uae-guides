Getting Your Ejari Certificate in Dubai: Every Method That Works in 2026

When you rent a flat or villa in Dubai, signing the tenancy contract is only the first step. The lease only becomes officially recognised once it is recorded in Ejari, the Dubai Land Department's tenancy registration system, run by RERA. Without it you cannot connect DEWA utilities, sponsor a family residence visa, or take a rental dispute to the authorities. Fortunately, the quickest way to register Ejari is online from your phone, usually finishing the same day.

What Ejari actually does for you

Ejari gives your tenancy contract legal standing. It is the record that proves you are the lawful tenant of the property for the stated period, and it is checked whenever you interact with Dubai's official services.

Your Ejari certificate and its unique contract number are what you will be asked for when you connect DEWA — a move-in is usually same-day once the Ejari is live, and the connection, security deposit and first bill all run off that number — as well as when you apply for or renew a residence visa, sponsor family members, open a bank account, or handle other official paperwork tied to your address. If you ever need to raise a rental dispute, Ejari registration is what gives you standing before the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre.

Method 1: Register Ejari online via the Dubai REST app

The Dubai REST app, the official app of the Dubai Land Department available on iOS and Android, is the cheapest route at AED 177.75 and the fastest for straightforward residential leases. It works best when the tenancy is between two individuals and the paperwork is in order. Follow these steps:

1. Sign the Unified Tenancy Contract. Registration is impossible without the standard DLD contract signed by both tenant and landlord. Attach any addenda you have agreed on, such as maintenance terms, pet rules, or early termination clauses.

2. Download the Dubai REST app and log in with UAE Pass. Both the tenant and the landlord need UAE Pass accounts, and both must be individuals. If a company manages the property, the company registers through the Ejari system instead.

3. Go to Services, then RERA, then Register Ejari Contract. This is the entry point for a new registration.

4. Enter the tenancy details. You will need the property unit, the annual rent, the contract start and end dates, and the details of both parties. If the property is already linked to the landlord's account, select it from the dashboard.

5. Upload the documents. Provide clear, readable scans of the signed tenancy contract, both parties' Emirates IDs, the title deed, and your DEWA premise number.

6. Submit and wait for the landlord's approval. If the tenant submits, the landlord gets a notification and approves it in the app. Chase them if they are slow — an unapproved request can eventually be cancelled.

7. Pay AED 177.75 online. Payment is by card, through Noqodi or the accepted payment methods offered in the app.

8. Download the Ejari certificate. It arrives digitally, both in the app and by email, as a PDF carrying your unique Ejari contract number. For complete and correct submissions, processing is typically same-day, sometimes within minutes to a few hours.

Either party can start the registration online, but the other must approve it. In practice, the tenant usually submits and the landlord approves in the app.

Method 2: Register at an authorised typing or trustee centre

If you would rather have someone handle the paperwork, authorised Real Estate Trustee Centres across Dubai — for example Al Tabu and Takhlees — register Ejari on your behalf. The steps mirror the online method, but a typist enters everything into the system for you.

Bring originals of all your documents: the signed tenancy contract, the tenant's and landlord's IDs, the title deed, and the DEWA premise number. The staff upload everything into the Ejari system and enter the tenancy details for you. You pay the fee at the counter, which is a little higher than the online route — around AED 220 according to current reporting, though you should confirm the exact figure at the counter. The DLD quotes roughly 25 minutes of processing time at trustee centres, excluding queue waiting time, and you receive your certificate the same day.

The document checklist

Missing paperwork is the number-one cause of delays. At minimum, keep ready:

  • The signed unified tenancy contract
  • The tenant's Emirates ID and passport
  • The landlord's ID or passport
  • The title deed, as proof of ownership
  • The DEWA premise number — the 9-digit number usually found on the property's door frame
  • A Power of Attorney, if someone is registering on behalf of the landlord or tenant

Scan or photograph documents clearly. Blurry uploads are one of the most common reasons online applications get rejected.

After registration: keeping your certificate

Your Ejari certificate is a digital PDF, and you can view and download it at any time in the Dubai REST app under RERA services. Keep it safe — the Ejari contract number on it is the reference number for your DEWA account, your visa applications, and any official address verification. The certificate is valid for the tenancy period stated on it.

One thing newcomers often miss: Ejari does not renew automatically. A fresh certificate must be registered for every tenancy period, even if you stay in the same flat with the same landlord. Start the renewal as soon as the renewal contract is signed and before the current certificate expires — the steps, documents and AED 177.75 online fee are identical to a first-time registration. A gap between the old certificate and the new one is a gap in your legal tenancy record, and it tends to surface at the worst moments: a DEWA query, a visa renewal, or a dispute.

Common reasons registration gets delayed

Delays almost always come from one of two sources: incomplete documents, or a landlord who has not approved the request yet. If you applied online, double-check that every upload is legible and complete, then nudge the landlord. At a trustee centre, the quoted processing time excludes the queue, so plan for some waiting.
