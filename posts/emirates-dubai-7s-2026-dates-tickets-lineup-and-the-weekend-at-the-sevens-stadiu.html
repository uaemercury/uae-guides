UAE MERCURY — Your Window to the Emirates

The last weekend of November in Dubai belongs to one event: the Emirates Dubai 7s. From Friday 27 to Sunday 29 November 2026, The Sevens Stadium on the Dubai–Al Ain Road turns into the Middle East's largest sports and entertainment festival, opening the HSBC SVNS 2026–27 season with three days of rugby, live music, family activities and fancy dress. Tickets have been on sale since 31 August 2026, and prices are unchanged for the third consecutive year. For the full dates, ticket prices, lineup and schedule, see the complete guide to the dubai sevens 2026 event.

What the Dubai 7s actually is

The Dubai 7s is two events in one. On the pitches, it is a world-class rugby sevens tournament: eight men's and eight women's national teams playing the HSBC SVNS, the sport's global series, plus a new SVNS 3 competition. Off the pitches, it is a festival — four music stages, food stalls, a kids' zone, netball, cricket, padel, Gaelic football and a WODON3 fitness competition. Six sports feature across the weekend, with Gaelic football making its debut in 2026.

That combination is why the event sells out most years. Plenty of ticket holders come for the music and the atmosphere and barely watch a match; plenty of others come for the rugby and stay for the concerts. The stadium site is large enough that the two crowds rarely get in each other's way.

Dates and the shape of the weekend

The event runs Friday 27 to Sunday 29 November 2026, and each day has a different character. Friday is Family Friday: Gulf, youth and invitational tournaments, youth finals, WODON3, the kids' zone and local bands and DJs. There is no international SVNS rugby on Friday — but the new SVNS 3 competition does start on Pitch 1, the first time international SVNS rugby has ever been played on a Friday in Dubai.

Saturday is HSBC SVNS day one, with men's and women's pool matches on Pitch 1, the SVNS 3 finals, fancy dress day, and a headliner DJ set on Beats on 2 in the evening. Sunday is finals day: SVNS knockouts, the finals and trophy presentation in the evening, the Gulf Men's final and the closing headliner set.

Gates open at 07:00 each day and last entry is 8pm, with no re-entry after 9pm. If you are going for the evening music, arriving in the afternoon is fine; if you want a good spot for the rugby, the morning pool matches are the time to be there.

Tickets and prices for 2026

Friday tickets cost AED 75, with under-15s free. Saturday and Sunday are AED 455 each, and Sunday tickets have limited availability. A three-day general admission pass costs AED 595, and a family three-day pass for two adults and two children costs AED 1,136. Groups of six or more adults can buy three-day passes at AED 446 per person.

Children's pricing on Saturday and Sunday is AED 40 for ages four to nine and AED 115 for ages ten to fourteen, while under-fours go free with registration. People of Determination tickets cost AED 375 including a carer. Weekend reserved seating runs from AED 695 for standard bays to AED 825 for premium bays, and hospitality options such as the Sunroom start at AED 1,925 per day.

HSBC cardholders get 20 percent off general admission for the first 800 tickets booked through the app, and Emirates Platinum cardholders have their own discount. There is no general promo code for 2026. Buy only through the official tickets site or the Emirates Dubai 7s app: entry is by digital QR code only, printed tickets are not accepted, screenshots do not scan, and tickets are non-refundable unless the event is cancelled.

The rugby: teams, pools and defending champions

New Zealand arrive as defending champions in both the men's and women's competitions, having beaten Australia in both 2025 finals. Australia's women had won five straight Dubai titles before New Zealand ended that run. The men's field is Argentina, Australia, Fiji, France, New Zealand, South Africa, Spain and the USA, split into two pools; the women's field is Australia, Canada, Fiji, France, Japan, New Zealand, Spain and the USA.

The opening fixtures on Saturday include New Zealand men against Fiji at 12:36pm and South Africa against Argentina at 11:52am. The new SVNS 3 tier adds 28 more teams and 70 matches in total across the whole event, which makes the Friday and Saturday schedules busier than in previous years.

Music: what is confirmed and what is not

Music runs across four stages: Frequency on 8, the main headliner stage on Pitch 8 after dark on Saturday and Sunday; Beats on 2, a raised DJ bar by Pitch 2; Rugby Rocks for tribute bands; and the Backyard and BrewFest stages for local and international tribute acts.

Confirmed so far are Melanie C and Jax Jones, both playing DJ sets on Beats on 2. The Frequency on 8 headliners had not been announced as of early November 2026, so do not trust any social media post naming them — the 2025 headliners, Sean Paul, Shaggy and FISHER, were last year's acts, not this year's. The full lineup is worth checking closer to the event, because the organisers have a habit of announcing big names late.

Getting there and practical tips

The Sevens Stadium sits on the Dubai–Al Ain Road (E66), beyond the end of the metro network, so plan transport in advance. Shuttle buses run from key points, taxis are plentiful but in high demand after the evening concerts, and driving means using the stadium parking with a long walk at the end of the night. The event is part of the same season as the city's big mass-participation weekends, and the Dubai Fitness Challenge is the wider festival it sits alongside.

Dress for a full day outdoors: comfortable shoes, sunscreen, and something warm for the evening, when the desert temperature drops after sunset. Fancy dress is a genuine tradition on Saturday, not a joke — half the crowd will be in costume. Bring ID, because bag checks and age checks are enforced, and remember that entry is by QR code in the app, not by a printed ticket. Book the Sunday ticket early if you want it: limited availability is stated on the official tickets page, and it is the day that sells out first.
