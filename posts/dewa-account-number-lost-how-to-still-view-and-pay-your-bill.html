UAE MERCURY — Your Window to the Emirates

Every DEWA service — checking a bill, paying it, querying it — asks for the same key: your 10-digit DEWA account number. Lose it and the digital doors close. The good news is that the number is recoverable in several ways, and you are rarely more than one document away from it. Here is the recovery ladder, easiest first.

1. Any old bill or email

DEWA's Green Bill lands in your email inbox every month, and the account number is printed at the top of every bill, paper or PDF. Search your inbox for "DEWA" before anything else. The same number appears on payment confirmations and receipts from your bank app if you have ever paid through it — check the beneficiary details you saved.

2. The DEWA app or website account

If you registered on the DEWA Smart App or website before, sign in — your premises and account number are saved in your profile even if the paper bills are long gone. Registration itself can be done with your Emirates ID, which is also how support staff can find your account when all else fails.

3. Your tenancy contract (Ejari)

Your DEWA account is created against your premises when you move in, and the account details are tied to your tenancy. Your landlord, real-estate agent or building management usually holds the DEWA account number for the unit — a single message to the agent who handed you the keys solves most lost-number cases in Dubai's rental market. If you activated DEWA yourself through the Ejari-linked "Move-in" service, the number was in the activation SMS and email.

4. Ask DEWA, with ID

DEWA's customer channels can locate your account using your Emirates ID and premises details (building name, apartment number, Makani number if you have it). This is the route when you have just moved, the previous tenant's bills keep arriving, or the account seems registered to someone else — a common handover wrinkle in rented homes that DEWA staff untangle daily.

Once you have it back

  • Save the number in your phone contacts as "DEWA Account".
  • Register it in your bank app as a saved beneficiary so it is stored somewhere you will never lose again.
  • Turn on the Green Bill and app notifications, so every future bill arrives with the number attached.

A note on whose bill it is

If the account turns out to be in a previous tenant's or the landlord's name, do not just pay and forget: get the account properly transferred or a new one opened for your tenancy, so deposits and liabilities are yours and only yours. Your deposit (typically AED 2,000 for an apartment) should sit against your own account.

A lost account number feels like a locked door. In practice it is a five-minute errand — the number is on your bills, in your inbox, at your agent's office and on DEWA's own system, waiting to be asked for.

Keep the number somewhere smarter

A final habit separates the organised from the anxious: the moment you recover the number, store it in three places — your phone contacts, your bank app's beneficiary list, and a note in the same folder as your tenancy contract. Account numbers are only ever "lost" once by people who do this. Everyone else loses them annually, usually on the day a bill is due.

Do it today, while the number is in front of you and the lesson is fresh. Future-you, standing at a payment screen on a deadline with a due bill and no account number, sends thanks in advance.
