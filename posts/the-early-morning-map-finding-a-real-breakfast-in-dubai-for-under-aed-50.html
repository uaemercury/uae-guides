Breakfast is the meal where Dubai's two price worlds collide most sharply. In a beach café in Jumeirah, a shakshuka or a smoothie bowl can cost AED 55 to 70 before coffee. Twenty minutes away in Karama, a masala dosa with sambar, chutney and a filter coffee costs less than AED 30, and in Bur Dubai a sit-down breakfast can start at AED 10. If you are starting work early, touring the old city, or simply trying to keep a daily food budget sane, the morning is the easiest meal of the day to get right — as long as you know which neighbourhood you are standing in.

The cheapest mornings are in Old Dubai

Deira, Bur Dubai and Karama hold the densest cluster of cheap breakfast kitchens in the city, and most of them open at 7am or earlier. South Indian breakfast is the best-value cooked start to the day: a dosa, idli and filter coffee is a full meal, served fast, and it rarely costs more than AED 30. At Saravanaa Bhavan in Karama, dosas start from around AED 17.50, and the mini tiffin — a sampler of several small dishes on one plate — solves the problem of not being able to decide. Kailash Parbat, at BurJuman and Al Seef, is cheaper still: individual breakfast items from around AED 10, unlimited chai refills, and an unlimited Indian breakfast advertised at AED 29 at the Al Seef branch on weekends. Raju Omlet, the Karama egg institution, serves a masala omelette at around AED 12 and egg bhurji with toast at AED 18; add a chai and the whole breakfast lands near AED 14. Queues form before 9am on weekends, which tells you what regulars think of it.

Deira adds a 24-hour option: ROAST at Business Village near the Clock Tower serves a full breakfast combo — eggs, sausage, hash brown, toast and fresh orange juice — for around AED 42 at literally any hour, which makes it the practical choice before an early flight or after a night shift.

The Emirati breakfast worth waking for

The most memorable cheap breakfast in Dubai is the traditional Emirati one, and it fits a modest budget if you order à la carte instead of a sharing tray. The classic dishes are balaleet — sweet vermicelli spiced with cardamom and cinnamon, served with an omelette — chebab pancakes with date syrup, soft khameer bread, and karak chai. At Arabian Tea House in the Al Fahidi Historical Neighbourhood, open since 1997 in a whitewashed courtyard under a wind tower, balaleet with omelette is around AED 36 and chebab bread around AED 34. The full Emirati tray at around AED 87 is built for sharing; split between two people it works out near AED 43 each, back inside budget. Go before 10am on weekends, because the shaded courtyard tables fill quickly. Al Fanar, the other heritage name, lists similar dishes at similar prices, with balaleet around AED 42.

Arabic breakfast: the quiet bargain

Levantine breakfast — foul, hummus, labneh, olives, fresh khubz and tea — is one of the best-value morning meals in the city, and the buffet version is cheaper still. Aurum in Discovery Gardens advertises an unlimited Lebanese breakfast buffet for AED 19, which makes it one of the cheapest breakfasts in Dubai full stop. Al Mallah in Satwa opens at 7am with manakish from around AED 10, and spots in Al Barsha and Dubai Silicon Oasis serve full Arabic spreads for around AED 25 to 30 a head. A foul with bread and tea in the right place costs less than AED 20 and keeps you full until lunch.

Even the Marina has two honest options

Dubai Marina is not budget territory, and a single à la carte dish at a Marina Walk café can break AED 50 on its own. But the area has two genuine exceptions, and both are set deals rather than menu orders. Vernos Café runs an unlimited breakfast buffet advertised at AED 45, with an egg station, sausages and pastries — remarkable value for the postcode. Lavash, at the Marina and in Jumeirah, serves a nine-dish international breakfast from AED 49, running from 8am to 4pm, which also makes it a late breakfast or early lunch. For something grab-and-go, a sausage, egg and cheese sandwich at a coffee chain costs around AED 23 — not a destination, but a reliable price in an expensive district. The Kerala buffet at Dhe Puttu, advertised at AED 18 from 7am to 11am, and the weekend vegetarian buffet at Swades around AED 21, complete the picture at the very bottom of the price scale.

For every place named here, with the dish to order and the price to expect, the full rundown of breakfast in Dubai under AED 50 groups the options by area and cuisine so you can pick by where you wake up.

Timing matters as much as place. Most of the old-city kitchens open at 7am and do their best business before 10am, so an early start means shorter queues and food straight off the stove. On Friday mornings, the week's busiest breakfast shift, either arrive early or expect a short wait — regulars treat the queue as part of the ritual, and the food at the end of it justifies the patience.

One last piece of local wisdom: the classic working Dubai breakfast is a karak chai at AED 1 to 3 and a manakish from AED 10. It is the cheapest start in the city, it is genuinely good, and once it becomes a habit, the AED 70 café breakfast starts to look like a very strange way to spend money.
