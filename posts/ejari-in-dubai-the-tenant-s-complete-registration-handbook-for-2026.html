Ejari in Dubai: The Tenant's Complete Registration Handbook for 2026

Signing a tenancy contract in Dubai is only half the job. Afterwards, the contract must be entered into the official registry — a step that sounds bureaucratic but unlocks everything else: your DEWA account, a family residence visa file, and any claim you might one day bring to the Rental Disputes Centre. Here is what it costs, which documents you need, and how to get it done without a typing-centre markup.

The registry is called Ejari — Arabic for "my rent" — the Dubai Land Department's official tenancy-contract system, administered by RERA. Every residential and commercial lease in Dubai must be recorded there, and once yours is entered you receive a certificate with a unique number and barcode: the legal proof of your tenancy.

Why every tenant ends up dealing with it

Without a registered tenancy, three ordinary things stop working. DEWA will not open an electricity and water account in your name. A family residence visa file cannot be submitted without an attested lease. The Rental Disputes Centre only accepts cases backed by a contract in the registry.

Legally the landlord must ensure registration, but in practice the tenant completes the process and pays — agree in writing, before you sign, who registers and who pays.

What registering actually costs

The cheapest official route is the Dubai REST app, where the total is AED 177.75. A detailed Ejari registration walkthrough breaks the figure down component by component:

  • Tenancy contract registration: AED 100
  • Knowledge fee: AED 10
  • Innovation fee: AED 10
  • Service partner fee: AED 55 online, AED 95 in person
  • VAT at 5% on the service partner fee only: AED 2.75 online, ~AED 4.75 in person

That gives AED 177.75 through the app and roughly AED 220 at a trustee centre. Renewal costs the same, and the figures exclude rent, security deposit, and agency commission.

Private online platforms are the priciest option — around AED 299 all-in in one published comparison. Government fees are reviewed from time to time, so always confirm the amount shown in the app before you pay.

The online route, step by step

The Dubai REST app is the route most tenants use: fast, cheap, and fully digital.

  1. Download the Dubai REST app and log in with UAE Pass. UAE Pass syncs your profile and Emirates ID automatically.
  2. Go to Services, then RERA, then Register Ejari Contract. The same service covers new registrations and renewals.
  3. Enter the 9-digit DEWA premises number. This pulls the correct property details into the form — double-check it against your contract. No actual bill is needed; the number alone is enough.
  4. Fill in the contract details. Lease dates, annual rent, number of cheques, and tenant and landlord information exactly as written in the signed contract.
  5. Upload your documents. The signed unified tenancy contract plus the documents below, as clear PDF or JPEG scans. Blurry uploads are the top cause of online rejection.
  6. Submit for landlord approval. The landlord approves through their own Dubai REST or DLD account; no approval within five days cancels the request, so nudge them the same day.
  7. Pay AED 177.75. By card or the app's accepted payment methods; the app shows the exact figure before you confirm.
  8. Download the certificate. Issued once approved and paid — usually the same day — and emailed to you as a PDF.

With complete documents, the certificate typically arrives within hours of approval and payment; if the landlord misses the five-day window, you resubmit.

If you would rather do it in person

Visit a trustee centre with the original signed unified tenancy contract and your Emirates ID — a representative also brings an official power of attorney. The agent files your details, you pay about AED 220, and the listed service time is roughly 25 minutes, excluding waiting time. Since late 2024, some trustees also accept registrations over WhatsApp: you share the documents digitally and the certificate comes back to you.

Documents to have ready

Gather everything first — a missing document is the main cause of delays:

  • The signed unified tenancy contract — original for in-person registration, a clear copy for the app. Only this official form is accepted.
  • The tenant's Emirates ID, plus passport copy and valid residence visa for non-GCC nationals.
  • The landlord's passport or Emirates ID copy and the property's title deed.
  • The DEWA premises number (or a recent DEWA bill).
  • A security deposit receipt, if one was issued.
  • A power of attorney, only if someone registers on behalf of the landlord or tenant.
  • A trade licence copy for commercial properties — a business cannot issue or renew its licence without a registered commercial lease.

The mistakes that get applications rejected

Most rejections are paperwork errors:

  • Blurry or cropped uploads. Scan in good light and check every page.
  • Mismatched property details. The premises number, unit number, and title deed must all describe the same property — one digit off sends you back to the start.
  • Expired documents. Check Emirates ID, visa, and passport expiry dates first.
  • The landlord never approves. The request dies after five days, so follow up the same day.
  • The wrong contract form. The DLD accepts only the unified tenancy contract.
  • Undeclared co-occupants. RERA requires co-occupant details to be declared for shared units.

That last point matters if you share your accommodation. If you rent a room or a bed space rather than a whole unit, the tenancy rules still apply — so read up on the bed space rules in Dubai before you sign.

After you have the certificate

Hand the Ejari number to DEWA to activate your electricity and water account — the app emails you an automatic activation link. Keep a PDF of the certificate with your passport and Emirates ID — needed for family visa applications, and the only version the Rental Disputes Centre will hear.

The registration does not renew automatically — register each new contract the same way, at the same fee, and start the renewal as soon as the new contract is signed to avoid gaps in your legal tenancy record. When you move out, cancel the record before the next tenant registers: free in the Dubai REST app, around AED 40 plus VAT at a trustee centre (confirm the figure at the counter).

Do it yourself through the app, keep your scans sharp, chase the landlord's approval the same day — AED 177.75, usually finished the same day.
